# Source — pages 905-923 (Olga's author notes)

**This file is source material, not analysis.** Everything below is the verbatim text of Olga
Makarova's `<div class="newText">` author-note block from each page of *Gifts of Wandering Ice*.
My interpretation of these blocks is in `../analysis/`, not here. Keeping the two separate is
part of the method: if source and reading are mixed, a reader cannot tell which iceberg they
are processing.

**How these were collected:** `https://giftscomic.com/index.php?comic_id=N` (N = page number + 1),
scraping the `newText` div. Collected 2026-09-15. Titles are the page titles as Olga gives them.

**Read the images, not only these notes.** See `image_urls.md`. The notes and the images diverge —
this is finding #4 in `../FINDINGS.md`.

## Page 907 — *Pearls and parasites*

**Olga's author note (verbatim):**

> Yes, this is exactly what pears are: entombed irritants, parasites and grains of sand that molluscs can't get rid of otherwise. Humans form similar (but not as beautiful) protective shells around grenade shards stuck in their bodies and sometimes embryos that died in their wombs. Some parasite larvae build their own calcium shells to protect shielmselves from the host's immune system and then hybernate there for decades, waiting for an opportunity. And so on, and so on... Nature is morbidly beautiful, isn't it?

## Page 914 — *The arrival of swifts*

**Olga's author note (verbatim):**

> The title of the encrypted archive is a reference to Page 235 with Elie's vision of flying with swifts. + Chapter 23 of "Hot Obsidian" is published. + GWI now has an Esperanto version . Esperanto is an artificial language you can learn in mere 10 hours. Compare it with at least 700 for English and 1000 for Russian (but that's for smart people: for example, since I'm not smart, it took me 23 years to learn English at a level, still poor, to be able to start writing GWI in it, and much more to even think of translating my novels). A dream to unite people of the world with an easy-to-learn international language is so beautiful that I fell in love with it right away and decided to make my contribution to the cause by translating GWI into Esperanto. It went suprisingly well. With only 25 pages online, the Esperanto version currently has more views per day than English and Russian versions combined. And I haven't even advertised.
