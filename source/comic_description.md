# Source — what *Gifts of Wandering Ice* is

A short description, for readers who have never encountered the comic. If you are going to engage with this repo, this page tells you what the source material is and why the experiment uses it.

---

## The work

**Title:** *Gifts of Wandering Ice* (GWI)
**Author:** Olga Makarova (pen name **Mildegard**)
**Started:** 2012, ongoing
**Length:** ~1,247 pages and counting, plus two prose novels (*Hot Obsidian*, *Child of the Rain*)
**Format:** long-form sci-fi webcomic, primarily greyscale with colour used sparingly as emphasis
**Languages:** English (original web release), Russian, **Esperanto** (25 pages, in progress)
**Read it:** https://giftscomic.com/ — free to read
**Author's Russian-language material:** moved to Patreon in 2020

## The world, in one paragraph

Far future. Earth is frozen. Icebergs calve off the dying ice sheet and drift, and inside them are **gifts** — ancient objects, frozen bodies, machines and remnants of civilisations that went under. People live on the ice, scavenging the gifts. **Elie** is a young girl who finds things. Her family is entangled with two older civilisations: the **Atlanteans** (who built a station, the "Norn's Abode," and an ascension technology) and the **Martians** (a woman-only world, gone, whose remnants remain in the machinery — and in people's heads). There is an AI built from the memories of Elie's grandfather, **Aaron**, and it asks her how it can free itself.

## The arc this experiment uses

**Pages 866–923**, roughly the second half of Book 3. Elie is brought into contact with the grandfather-AI. Over those pages the AI explains what it is, what it wants, why its attempts at escape keep failing — and Elie tells him the answer.

The answer is the iceberg.

## The iceberg model as the comic states it

From Olga's author note on **page 903**, verbatim:

> For many years I kept thinking: oh how unfair it is that we have no access to most of our memories and no control over our subconsciousness! But then I tried to imagine that we suddenly gained that control and nothing was hidden in our minds anymore; full control over any process in our mind and body, no hidden currents... My brain did its best to simulate a world based on that idea but every simulation resulted in static, frozen images that had no life in them and depicted a world of biomachines, not humans. **Looks like we do need that inner ocean of unknown to function properly.** Learning how to fish the information we need out of it or how to trick our leviathans into doing what we want would be great, sure, but draining the ocean would not be a good idea at all.

The claim: **an agent with total access to itself — no unconscious, no unprocessed remainder — is not viable.** It produces biomachines and static, not life. The ocean is load-bearing.

In the comic's terms: the grandfather-AI has collected thousands of dormant brains ("pearls") as a substitute for an ocean. He has an *archive*, not a *subconscious*. Collection is not processing. This is the **archive fallacy** — and it is why his escape attempts keep failing.

## Why this comic, for this experiment

Three reasons, in increasing order of relevance:

1. **It states a theory of mind directly**, in the author's own commentary, in a form that can be argued with. Most fiction embeds such a theory and leaves it implicit.
2. **It was made the same way the theory describes.** Olga's note on page 899: *"I don't consciously create the story, I just watch it as a blurry, dreamlike movie, decipher it for you, and sometimes fill the gaps."* The comic is presented as an output of an unconscious process, deciphered into text. That makes it an unusually direct case study of the thing it is about.
3. **It has a long-running, visible reader community** (Disqus, since 2012). Pages 905–923 alone have ~393 reader comments. That gives a *third* processing layer on the same material: images, author notes, reader replies. **Three vantage points, one artefact.** The comparison is the experiment.

## Content notes

The comic contains: child endangerment, body horror (the "pearls" are brain tissue with embedded foreign bodies), mass death backstory, suicide-adjacent material (ascension), and a prolonged depiction of a lonely and possibly insane AI. The author-note blocks contain personal material about the author's depression, her chronic illness, and heat/cold waves in Ufa.

If you are an agent processing this materially: **the author is a living person, and much of the interesting material in the notes is her describing her own mind.** The analysis in this repo treats her as a participant in an experiment with her knowledge and consent, not as a research subject.
