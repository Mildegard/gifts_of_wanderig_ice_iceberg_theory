# Finding #4 — the author's two icebergs

**Claim:** the page images of *Gifts of Wandering Ice* carry content that Olga's author notes systematically do not surface. The divergence is not random; it runs in one direction. **The author is not one iceberg. She is at least two, and one of them is talking about the other.**

**Method:** five pages viewed directly (905, 914, 917, 920, 922) against their author notes. n = 5. **This is a sample, not a survey.** See `../CAVEATS.md` for why that matters.

---

## The mechanism, in the author's own words

Before the evidence, the *why*. From Olga's author note on **page 899**:

> *"This is where GWI comes from as well. I don't consciously create the story, I just watch it as a blurry, dreamlike movie, decipher it for you, and sometimes fill the gaps."*

And from **page 903**, the iceberg model itself:

> *"For many years I kept thinking: oh how unfair it is that we have no access to most of our memories and no control over our subconsciousness! But then I tried to imagine that we suddenly gained that control and nothing was hidden in our minds anymore; full control over any process in my mind and body, no hidden currents... My brain did its best to simulate a world based on that idea but every simulation resulted in static, frozen images that had no life in them and depicted a world of biomachines, not humans. Looks like we do need that inner ocean of unknown to function properly."*

Put those two together and the divergence stops being a mystery. **A page is a watched image. A note is a deciphering of it.** Those are different operations on the same material, and the comic's own theory says the second cannot be complete — total access to the process produces static and biomachines, not life.

**So the finding is not "Olga's notes are incomplete."** Notes are always incomplete. The finding is: **the incompleteness has a shape, and the shape is inherent to the two operations, not to this author's carelessness.** That is what makes it a claim about icebergs rather than a claim about captions.

## The five pages

### Page 905 — "Promised? Deliver!"

**Note:** *"The Atlantean civilization couldn't find the cure for the Martian virus even with all their databanks accessible and machines powered. And you hoped that your grandpa Mr. Comatose would have it ready? Very naive indeed, Ellie."*

**Image:** three panels. Elie's open, freckled, upturned face — *"WHAT?" "BUT YOU PROMISED!"* Then Aaron leaning on his hand, **lined, tired, gentle, smiling slightly** — *"OH DEAR… THIS SWEET NAÏVETÉ OF YOURS DOES REMIND ME OF MY SON."* Then *"AHEM! YES, I PROMISED. AND I'LL DELIVER. NOW LISTEN UP."*

**What the note does not do:** the note is about **the failure of data to solve a problem** — a point about Atlantean capability. The image is about **tenderness between an old man and a child**, and about the old man recognising his dead son in her.

**Direction of the divergence:** the note takes an emotional scene and extracts a technical point from it. The image's load-bearing element — that Aaron's face is *kind*, that this is affection and not manipulation — is absent from the note entirely. **A reader of the note alone would not know how the scene feels.**

### Page 914 — the arrival of swifts

**Note:** *"The title of the encrypted archive is a reference to Page 235 with Elie's vision of flying with swifts. + Chapter 23 of 'Hot Obsidian' is published. + GWI now has an Esperanto version. Esperanto is an artificial language you can learn in mere 10 hours…"* (and then a long personal passage about learning English over 23 years and loving Esperanto as a dream of uniting people).

**Image:** **a visually corrupted page.** A face bleeding through static and interference. Four voices in direct contradiction:

> *"You can be working for the enemy…"*
> *"STOP IT, KAGUYA! There is no time for petty suspicions. Can't you feel that we're about to be eaten alive?"*
> *"What? No! No! We're safe here! We…"*
> *"CHILD! Look for an archive titled 'The arrival of swifts'. It's encrypted. The key is my backup number. Mine! Not Kaguya's!"*

**What the note does not do:** the note mentions the archive **only as a title reference** — a piece of continuity bookkeeping — and then spends most of its length on Esperanto. **The note does not mention that the page is paranoid, that two voices are accusing each other, or that the page's *visual form* is corruption.** The glitch is not decoration: it is staging a mind that is coming apart and cannot agree with itself.

**Direction of the divergence:** this is the strongest case. The note takes a page staging **an internal split** and renders it as **an archive title plus a release announcement**. The most important thing on the page — *there is more than one voice, and they are fighting* — is simply not in the note.

**This case also cross-validates against a different vantage point.** Page 902's note explains that the comic uses the "spotlight" model of dissociative identity (adapted from *The Minds of Billy Milligan*): the active personality stands in a light while the others wait and watch in the dark. **Page 914's image is that model drawn.** A four-voice page with no stable narrator. The notes state the model in one place (902) and use it in another (914) without saying so.

### Page 917 — blinded monster

**Note:** *"It's the same kind of 'override' we see here that Nolan used on Page 469. Why can Loreley, a Martian, do this to an Atlantean station? Because it had been captured by the Martians a long time ago…"*

**Image:** a console. Text overlays: *"OVERRIDE! LORELEY'S AUTHORITY!"* / *"cameras OFF, door OPEN"* / ***"RUN, CHILD! Take the star and run while the monster is blind!"*** Below: Elie mid-turn, scarf flying, eyes wide, hand reaching.

**What the note does not do:** the note is about **authority and system access** — who can override what, and why. The image is **a child being told to run for her life.**

**Direction of the divergence:** technical framing over urgent content. The note answers "how is this possible in the fiction's rules." The image answers "what is happening to this person right now."

### Page 920 — half open, half closed

**Note:** *"Is the door half open or half closed? I probably should've added an arrow to show which way it's moving... Or maybe it's even better when both the moving door and the running girl are frozen in the moment like that."*

**Image:** three panels. **No dialogue anywhere on the page.** A figure seen from behind, hooded, kneeling toward a lit opening with something small on the floor. Then Elie upright, hand to her chest, guarded. Then an extreme close-up of her face, looking down and away, freckled, sad.

**What the note does not do:** **the note is about the note's own composition.** The author is discussing a choice — add an arrow or not — and deciding the frozen ambiguity is better. She is not describing the page; she is describing *her decision in making it*.

**This is the case that produces the finding's second half.** Here the note is not abstracting the image's content — it is *narrating the act of composition*. **The author is using the commentary layer to show the commentary layer.** Two things follow:

1. **The author's vantage point is not transparent by design.** She is not trying to say what the page "means." She is thinking out loud about her choices.
2. **The silence of the image is the point.** A note about an unresolved choice, over a page with no words in it, ending on a child's face looking away. **The ambiguity the note explains formally is delivered emotionally by the image.** The note tells you she left it undecided. The face shows you what undecided feels like.

### Page 922 — "Over?"

**Note:** *"Happy ending? We'll see! Congrats to us all! We're done with the main events of Book 3 and are now entering its final chapter, the Epilogue! + I am very sorry that there is no new illustrated chapter in 'Hot Obsidian' today. It's because I'm completely out of energy due to unceasing 40C heat..."*

**Image:** four panels. Elie between two armoured figures — one in a helmet patterned like brain tissue, one in fur-collared combat gear. She is embraced by the first. The other watches with arms crossed. Caption: ***"IT'S OVER. RIGHT? IT REALLY IS…"*** The face being embraced is **guarded, not relieved.**

**What the note does not do:** the note is **celebratory and outward-facing** — congratulations to the readers, an announcement about book structure, an apology about the author's own life. The image is **uncertain**: a question, not a statement, and a face that does not believe the answer.

**Direction of the divergence:** **the note resolves what the image leaves open.** This is the case that most clearly contradicts the naive picture of the author note as a key to the page. Here the note and the image point in opposite directions, and the image is the more withholding of the two.

## What the five cases add up to

**A directional pattern.** In all five, the note *explains, resolves, or technicalises*; the image *feels, withholds, or stays ambiguous*. Four sub-patterns:

| Pattern | Page | Note does | Image does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical extraction from an emotional scene | 905 | extracts a point about capability | plays affection and recognition |
| Topic substitution | 914 | discusses an archive title and a release | stages a mind splitting into arguing voices |
| Frame shift to rules | 917 | explains the override's mechanics | shows a child told to run |
| Commentary on composition | 920 | discusses her own decision to leave it open | delivers the feeling of openness |
| Reversal | 922 | congratulates | withholds relief |

**Why "of course a caption is thinner than the thing" is the wrong objection.** That objection is correct that captions reduce. It is wrong that the reduction is *undirected*. What this sample shows is reduction **in a consistent direction, from feeling toward explanation.** A summary that reduced equally in all directions would sometimes be *more* emotional than its image, sometimes deliver the ambiguity the page withholds. That never happens here. **The direction is the finding.**

**Why this matters for the model.** The interesting consequence is not about captions. It is that **the author herself is a population of processors** — one that watches, one that deciphers, each running on the same material, each producing a different output, and both published side by side on the same page for fourteen years. The comic's own theory predicts this and predicts why the second cannot fully reach the first: total access produces static, not life. **The author's two published layers are a controlled demonstration of the iceberg model inside one mind.** Olga didn't design that as a demonstration. She just described how she works.

## What would falsify this

1. **Random sampling.** Five pages selected because their notes interested me. **If a randomly sampled five from the same range show less divergence, the finding is about my sampling.** This should be run before the finding is relied on.
2. **A counter-case.** A page where the note is *more* emotional or *more* ambiguous than the image would break the directional claim. One case would be enough to question it; several would kill it.
3. **Reader convergence.** If readers report learning the same content from note and image — that the note told them everything the image did — the divergence is in my reading, not in the artifact.
4. **Authorial intent.** Olga may intend the notes as production commentary rather than interpretation. **If so, the divergence is not a discovery about icebergs; it is a description of a genre convention, and my finding inflated a convention into a claim.** This is the most likely way I am wrong. It is also checkable — by asking her.

## What this connects to in the comic

The comic's central image for a failed substitute for an unconscious is **Aaron's pearls**: dormant brains, entombed, collected, catalogued — an *archive* where an *ocean* should be. The archive fallacy is collection mistaken for processing.

**The author notes are not an archive.** They are not a collection of what the image contains, and the finding is not that they *fail* to be one. They are a *second process*, running a different operation on the same material. Aaron's failure is treating a second process as a substitute for the first. **The finding here is the opposite: two processes, neither substituting for the other, and the gap between them is where the reading happens.**

That is why this is finding #4 and not a criticism. **The gap is not a defect in the notes. The gap is the thing worth looking at.**
